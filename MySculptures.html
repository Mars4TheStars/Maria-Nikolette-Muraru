My Sculptures

Everything is in your hand(s) - project video

if the video player isn't working press the play button

I create because I need to—if I don’t, my brain feels like it “glitches.” There’s a sense of urgency in externalizing ideas, not just to feel accomplished but to keep my thoughts moving. A lot of times even when a thought will never be realised, such as changing the layout of your house, due to financial constraints, I just want to think about it, how I would go about it, what I would use, etc. This project reflects the need to make something just for the sake of it, combining color, movement, and interactivity in a way that invites curiosity and play.

Inspired by both carousels and color wheels, the piece allows for interchangeable images that overlap and shift when powered on. Light components and animation further enhance the experience. A button, “shaped” like a hand, serves as the power switch—making activation a physical interaction between the viewer and the work.

Color has always been a central part of what I make. It ties back to childhood, carrying a nostalgic weight that informs my aesthetic choices. While I’m drawn to the process of assembling and experimenting, the challenge lies in cohesion—deciding how to bring everything together in a way that feels intentional, even when working with scrap materials. As it is such a challenge sometimes working with scraps and figuring out their use in the appropriate circumstances. Having a constraint that you have to work around and when you do, feel so accomplished. Plus, I’m giving these items most likely a second life, second meaning.

Beyond the mechanics, I want this project to make people think. Even if it appears playful or lighthearted, I believe that complex ideas are often best approached through a sense of fun. I tend to overanalyze everything, spiraling down rabbit holes of thought, and I hope my work encourages audiences to do the same—to look, question, and interpret in their own way. I like feeling useful in making people think.

The battle of heart and brain

if the video player isn't working press the play button

I'm creating this project to explore the themes of certainty and indecisiveness—both in how we react to external influences and in the constant balance between heart and mind. To me, this project reflects a personal exploration of how media and external stimuli impact our emotional and mental states, often leading us to question our own beliefs or decisions.

The bear's changing colors depending on the TV's state symbolizes how we, too, experience shifts in mood, passion, or clarity based on what we're consuming or experiencing. When the TV is on, emotions are monotonous but heightened, which is why the heart is red, and the mind is lulled into passivity which is why it's green; However, when the TV is off, the heart finds calm, which is why a happy go lucky melody plays; yet the mind is in more of an alert mode (red). This visual interplay between the "heart" and "brain" represents a struggle for internal balance that I often feel—trying to maintain a sense of certainty and groundedness in a world full of noise and distractions.

This project gives me a way to express and question how much our inner states are our own and how much they are shaped by what we allow in from the outside. It’s a tangible representation of how subtle changes in our environment can lead to profound shifts in our inner worlds, making us question: What is truly ours, and what is simply a reaction to what surrounds us?
